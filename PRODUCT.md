# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Curious music fans in a moment of casual discovery. Someone hears an artist —
in a café, a film, a conversation — and wants to quickly know their biggest
song, their most-played record, and who else sounds like them. The context is
idle curiosity and quick lookups, frequently on a phone, and the reward they're
after is an instant, satisfying answer plus a nudge toward what to play next.

## Product Purpose

TopTracks answers one question well: given any artist or band, what's their
**most played track**, their **most played album**, and **three similar
artists** — with real artwork attached. Data comes from Last.fm (the ranking
source of truth) and Spotify (artwork only). Success is not a single answer
delivered and forgotten; it's the user **kept exploring** — one lookup leading
to the next through the similar-artists path, turning a search into a discovery
loop.

## Positioning

The fastest, warmest way to go from an artist's name to their biggest track and
the very next thing worth hearing. Not a catalog, not a streaming app — a
single, focused answer that always offers a doorway to the next one.

## Brand Personality

Warm, playful, inviting. The voice is human and expressive — discovery should
feel like a friend's recommendation, not a database query. This is an intent
worth calling out against the current build, which leans refined, dark, and
atmospheric; future design work should carry the atmosphere the app already has
toward something more welcoming and alive, without tipping into loudness.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboard**: no identical card grids, hero-metric big-number
  tiles, or navy-and-white corporate sterility. This is not a tool that reports;
  it's a tool that delights.
- **Sterile, flat, generic**: never so minimal it loses character — no
  unbranded gray, no atmosphere-free default components.
- **Loud, neon, maximalist**: no gratuitous glow, motion overload, or
  glassmorphism-as-decoration. Warmth is not the same as noise.

## Design Principles

Answer first. The top track is the headline; the result should land instantly
and read unambiguously as track first, then album, then similar artists. Speed
and clarity are the product.

Every answer is a doorway. The similar-artists path is not a footnote — it's the
engine of the discovery loop. Design should always make the next search feel
inviting and one tap away.

Warm, not corporate. Favor a human, recommending-a-friend tone in copy and
feel over efficient-but-cold product convention. Personality is a feature here,
not a risk.

Character with clarity. Keep the editorial identity and atmosphere that make the
app memorable, but never at the expense of legibility, hierarchy, or task speed.
When the two conflict, the task wins.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA. Hold the dark, tinted palette to ≥4.5:1 body contrast
(scrutinize the muted and faint text tokens especially), keep visible focus
states and full keyboard navigability, and honor `prefers-reduced-motion` — the
reveal, equalizer, and shimmer animations all need a calm alternative.
